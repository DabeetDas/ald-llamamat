RF N2 plasmas are known to create predominantly molecular N2 ions such as N2+ and some atomic nitrogen species such as N and N+. We believe that atomic N and N+ are the key growth species. In this case, atomic N radicals exit the plasma and diffuse across the gas phase boundary layer and adsorb on the wafer surface. The N+ (and N2+) ions instead are accelerated across the plasma sheath where they either adsorb or elastically/inelastically scatter from the wafer surface. The adsorbed species react to form Si–N. In addition, N+ ions probably also collide with enough energy to break some surface Si–Si and Si–N bonds that leads to increased Si–N bond formation and film densification. This may partially explain the slow saturation in growth rate with N2 plasma time observed for the exposure times investigated. It is also believed that atomic N and N+ and N2+ ions react with Si–H surface bonds and abstract hydrogen from the growth surface. As shown in Fig. 9(a), the N2 plasma step is believed to generate a nitrogen terminated surface with several nitrogen dangling bonds.
