  "evidence": "The ZrO2 particles initially displayed vibrational modes consistent with ZrOH* surface species. TiCl4 exposure at 600 K removed the surface hydroxyls and subsequent H2O exposure at 600 K produced TiOH* surface species. The occurrence of this reaction would be further supported by the appearance of Ti–Cl stretching vibrations. The difference spectra for the initial two TiCl4/H2O reaction cycles are shown in Fig. 1. These spectra are displaced for clarity in presentation. The spectra in Figs. 1a and 2 are consistent with TiCl4 reacting with hydroxyl groups on the initial ZrO2 surface according to the following reaction: xZrOH* + TiCl4 -> ZrOTiCl* + xHCl (4). The hydroxyl groups observed on the ZrO2 particles prior to TiO2 ALD were consistent with ZrOH* species on a tetragonal ZrO2 surface. These hydroxyls have broad O–H stretching vibrational features at 3650 and 3730 cm-1. During the first two AB cycles shown in Fig. 1, the intensity of the ZrO–H stretching vibrational feature declines at 3650 cm-1. After several TiCl4/H2O cycles, a prominent feature is observed in Figs. 1 and 3 at 3734 cm-1. After nine and 10 TiCl4/H2O cycles, Figs. 4, 6 and 7 reveal that this absorbance at 3734 cm-1 dominates the O–H stretching vibration spectra. The sharp peak at 3734 cm-1 is also accompanied by a broad shoulder of low intensity in the range of 3600–3720 cm-1. There are several reported frequencies in this region for hydroxyls on both anatase and rutile TiO2 surfaces. An additional broad feature of low intensity was observed centered at 3400 cm-1. This feature has been assigned to the O–H stretch of hydrogen bonded hydroxyls on a rutile TiO2 surface. The hydroxyls on the TiO2 particles of this study were created at 600 K (327 8C). The correspondence between the sharp absorbance peak at 3734 cm-1 and the absorbance peaks at 3730–3740 cm-1 for rutile TiO2 surfaces argues that the TiOH* surface species observed in this study reside on rutile TiO2 surfaces."
