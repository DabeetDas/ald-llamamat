The barrier properties against Cu diffusion for the as-deposited thin films (less than 10 nm) grown at 225 and 275 °C are analyzed using ex-situ X-ray diffraction (XRD) and electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). EIS helps to determine the failure of the MoNx barrier layer qualitatively by comparing different EI spectra obtained after annealing at different temperatures. Both XRD and EIS analyses clearly show that the ALD-MoNx film deposited at the higher ALD temperature makes a better Cu diffusion barrier layer. The film thicknesses of the as-grown films were measured by X-ray reflectance (XRR) and cross-sectional view field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM). The phases and crystallinities of the as-grown and annealed MoNx thin films were analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD). The sheet resistances of the films deposited at different temperatures were measured using a four-point probe. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to confirm the elemental distributions and corresponding oxidation states of the films. Cross-sectional view transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis for the Cu/MoNx/Si structure was performed before the diffusion-barrier-related experiments. Electrical impedance spectra (EIS) were recorded in three different modes. The resistivity of the MoNx films decreases sharply with deposition temperature and is significantly reduced further upon post-annealing. The GPC of the MoNx thin film exhibits self-saturation beyond a reactant pulsing time of 3 s, whereas a precursor pulsing duration of 5 s (or larger) is required to maintain an almost constant GPC. The film thicknesses were measured using XRR. The compositions of the MoNx thin films were estimated from the XPS analysis as a function of the argon-ion etch time (XPS depth profile analysis). The SIMS analysis of the MoNx samples deposited at 275 °C is performed. The SIMS confirms the uniform molybdenum and nitrogen throughout the depth of the sample.
