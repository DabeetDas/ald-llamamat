  "evidence": "Imai et al.17 have demonstrated the Si–ALE using cyclic exposures of dichlorosilane (DCS; SiH2Cl2) as a self-limiting precursor and atomic hydrogen, H, as a reducer. Yarmoff et al.15 have shown that silicon monochloride (SiCl) is only one surface species which is stable on the Si (100) and Si(111) surfaces in the temperature range between room temperature and 800 °C. Coon et al.27 have also shown that DCS adsorbs dissociatively on the Si (111) surface by forming SiCl, H, and Cl surface adsorbates. Sugahara et al.23 have analyzed the Si–ALE characteristics based on these backgrounds, and have predicted that the ideal growth rate of 1 monolayer (ML) per cycle is achieved only when monochlorosilylene (MCS; SiHCl) predominates the other precursors having two Cl atoms, such as DCS or dichlorosilylene (SiCl2) since one of Cl atoms of these precursors adsorbs directly on the clean Si surface, resulting in 0.5 ML coverage of deposited SiCl. Predominant radicals formed by thermal dissociation of DCS are said to be SiCl2,28–31 since it has the smallest free energy change. However, MCS is also a possible product by the gas-phase reaction of DCS. 32–34 For Si–ALE, especially, many collisions of molecules are expected in the gas-phase since the chamber pressure is as high as 1022 Torr. The high pressure will result in the gas-phase generation of MCS, a desirable precursor."
