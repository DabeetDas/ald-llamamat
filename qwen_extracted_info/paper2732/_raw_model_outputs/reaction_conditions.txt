  "evidence": "During the very first cycles the film growth proceeds according to the ideal ALE mechanism, i.e., 1 ML of CuCl, or the corresponding oligomers Cu_xClx, is first adsorbed on the substrate surface and, subsequently, this monolayer reacts with the incoming zinc atoms resulting in elemental copper and volatile ZnCl2. However, once the first copper nuclei have been formed during the first cycles, the dissolution of zinc into them starts to complicate the process. When dosed on copper nuclei covered with 1 ML of adsorbed CuCl, Zn first reduces CuCl as desired, but rather than saturatively adsorbing on the surface, a part of the excess Zn dissolves into the Cu nuclei forming a Cu–Zn alloy. After the termination of the zinc pulse, the Cu–Zn alloy tries to equilibrate itself with the gas phase. This results in an outdiffusion of Zn, i.e., Eq. (2) proceeds now from right to the left. Due to the constant purging of the reactor with an inert gas, no equilibrium is reached and the outdiffusion of zinc occurs continuously, also during the subsequent CuCl pulse. This leads into a situation where both Zn and CuCl are present on the surface and in the gas phase. As a consequence, CVD-like reactions take place thereby causing a loss of the self-limitation of the copper deposition."
