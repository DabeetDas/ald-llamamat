  "evidence": "Twenty bilayers of titanium oxide and aluminum oxide with the layer-pair thickness of 4.43 nm on a silicon substrate were fabricated for novel multilayer reflectors at water-window wavelengths by an atomic layer deposition method of controlled growth with sequential surface chemical reactions. Tetrachlorotitanium (TCT) and H2O2 were used as vapor sources for the titanium oxide films. Trimethylaluminum (TMA) and H2O2 were used as vapor sources for the aluminum oxide films. The growth rates were as low as 0.1 nm/cycle in the substrate temperature range of 140–700 °C. The experimental setup used a stainless-steel vacuum chamber, three computer-controlled leak valves, a turbomolecular pump, and an infrared-ray heater. The growth rate of titanium oxide films increased with substrate temperature up to 340–490 °C, and the growth rate decreased at over 490 °C. The study demonstrated high reflectance of over 30% at 2.734 nm and an incident angle of 71.8° from normal incidence."
