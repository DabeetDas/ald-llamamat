  "summary": "The study investigates the electrical properties of high-permittivity oxides such as HfO2, Ta2O5, and Nb2O5 nanolaminates deposited on silicon substrates using atomic layer deposition (ALD). The research focuses on the interface quality, defect density, and electrical characteristics of these materials. The study compares the performance of different precursors and growth conditions, highlighting the impact of growth temperature and precursor chemistry on the electrical properties and defect densities of the films. The results show that HfO2 films grown from HfCl4 on HF-etched silicon exhibit the lowest interface trap density, while Ta2O5 and Nb2O5–Ta2O5–Nb2O5 films show higher defect densities.   "evidence": "The oxides studied included HfO2 film grown from two different oxygen-free metal precursors and Ta2O5 and Nb2O5 nanolaminates. The interface trap densities correlated to the oxide growth chemistry and semiconductor substrate treatment. No gap state densities induced by structural disorder were measured in the films grown on chemical SiO2. Trap densities were also clearly lower in HfO2 films compared to Ta2O5–Nb2O5. HfO2 films were deposited using two representative hafnium precursors selected from hafnium alkylamides and hafnium halides. The characteristics of HfO2-based films were compared to the Ta2O5 and Nb2O5 films grown using ethoxides of the respective metals. The HfO2 films were grown at 205, 235, and 300 8C from Hf[N(CH3)(C2H5)]4 onto the Si(100) substrate covered with chemically grown 1.2- to 1.8-nm-thick SiO2 layer. The HfO2 films were also grown from HfCl4 at 300 8C, but onto Si(100) substrate etched in HF aqueous solution (1%) for 0.5 min in order to remove native SiO2. The Ta2O5 films were grown from Ta(OC2H5)5 and H2O at 300 8C on HF-etched p-Si(100), using Ta precursor pulse length of 0.2 s and water pulse length of 2.0 s. The same parameters were used for Nb2O5 growth. For Ta2O5, 235 growth cycles were applied giving the eventual thickness of 7.5 nm. In the case of Nb2O5–Ta2O5–Nb2O5 trilayer, 42 cycles were applied to grow the first Nb2O5 layer, followed by 165 cycles of Ta2O5, capped again with 42 cycles for Nb2O5 growth. The study has been aimed at the comparative examination of differently devised capacitor structures, using exactly the same technique for all the samples, while the defects affecting their performance and measured in this study were induced by the disorder in the oxide and in the vicinity of oxide/substrate interface."
