As was given in Fig. 2, the film growth rate is constant irrespective of deposition temperature, therefore thickness of the films grown by 2000 cycles have the same thickness as shown in TEM micrographs in Fig. 6 for the films deposited at 350 o and 500 o C. It is found that the electrical resistivity decreases with the deposition temperature, a rapid decrease from 350 o C to 450 o C and a slow decrease from 450 o C to 500 o C. The electrical resistivity obtained for the TiN film deposited at 500 o C was as low as 72×10 −6 Ω cm. The reason for the variation of the electrical resistivity with the deposition temperature seems to be density of the film. It was found that a distinct difference of the films deposited at low temperature and high temperature was their preferred orientation. As shown in Fig. 8, XRD patterns revealed that TiN films were grown in [111] direction above 450 o C and in the [100] direction below 450 o C. Since the (111) plane is the densest plane in the TiN crystal, having a rock salt structure, and the thickness of the two films are the same, it is expected that the film grown in the [111] direction has a denser structure and hence lower electrical resistivity."
