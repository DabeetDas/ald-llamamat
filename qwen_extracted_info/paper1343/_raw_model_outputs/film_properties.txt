CONCLUSIONS
The results obtained in this work demonstrate that substrate biasing during PEALD can be used
to tune the properties of oxide and nitride thin-films. The application of substrate biasing during
plasma exposure steps can influence the energy and dose of ions impinging on the film surface,
thereby enabling the tuning of a wide range of material properties. The results also show that
substrate biasing can be used to enable the formation of crystalline material at low temperatures
which would otherwise yield amorphous films for deposition without biasing. This is especially
important for applications where high temperature processing is not feasible. Furthermore, the
results demonstrate that substrate biasing can be used to enable topographically selective
deposition on 3D substrates, which is highly desirable for applications such as microelectronics,
optoelectronics and sensors. The ability to control the crystalline phase of the deposited film by
varying the ion energy and dose during PEALD could also be used to tailor the optical, electrical
and mechanical properties of the film. This is especially important for applications where the
properties of the film need to be different at different regions of the 3D substrate. The results
obtained in this work provide a foundation for further research on the use of substrate biasing
during PEALD for the fabrication of advanced devices and for the development of new materials
with tailored properties.
