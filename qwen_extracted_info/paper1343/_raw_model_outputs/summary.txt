CONCLUSIONS
The results obtained in this work demonstrate that substrate biasing during PEALD can be used
to tune a wide range of material properties of oxide and nitride thin-films. The effects of ion
surface interactions during plasma exposure can be controlled by varying the magnitude and
duration of the applied bias. This can be used to achieve topographically selective deposition on
3D substrates. The results also show that the implementation of substrate biasing during PEALD
can provide an alternative route for obtaining crystalline materials at low temperatures, which
would otherwise be amorphous. This is especially useful for materials that require high
temperatures for crystallization, such as SiOx and SiNx, where the use of substrate biasing can
avoid the need for high temperature annealing steps. Furthermore, the results demonstrate that
substrate biasing during PEALD can be used to improve the uniformity of films on large-area
substrates. This is especially important for the semiconductor industry where uniformity is a
critical requirement for device performance. The results also show that substrate biasing during
PEALD can be used to improve the properties of certain materials, such as TiOx, HfOx, TiNx and
HfNx, by enabling the formation of different material phases at different surface orientations
during the same deposition run. This is in line with the concept of topographically selective
deposition proposed by Kim et al. 3 and is also in agreement with the results reported by other
researchers for topographically selective deposition on 3D substrates.71
