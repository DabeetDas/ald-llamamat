  "evidence": "It is well known that La2O3 easily reacts with ambient moisture to become lanthanum hydroxide, La(OH)3, which is chemically stable. It is speculated that during the H2O pulse, the La–OH forms on the surface and turns to La(OH)3. This transition will be more prominent when the H2O pulse length is longer. It is believed that the formation of La(OH)3 on the surface will hinder further reaction with La precursor and thus result in a decrease of the deposition rate. However, it is a different situation in HfLaOx alternate deposition. As the La precursor flows, the surface is covered by Hf–OH. There is no problem of reaction between the La precursor and Hf–OH. Thus, lanthanum is deposited on the surface without any inhibition. Further, during the H2O pulse, immediately following the presence of hafnium on the surface reduces the formation of La(OH)3 and results in dangling La–OH molecules remaining on the surface. As the Hf precursor flows, the dangling La–OH reacts with the Hf precursor."
