C. Chemisorption mechanisms
In ALD, the chemisorption of the reactant molecules is
typically the rate-determining step. The chemisorption
mechanism is therefore of central importance for the
understanding of the surface chemistry of ALD. The
chemisorption of the reactant molecules can be divided into
two main types: the direct chemisorption and the indirect
chemisorption. In direct chemisorption, the reactant
molecules chemisorb directly on the surface. In indirect
chemisorption, the reactant molecules chemisorb via an
intermediate species. The intermediate species may be
either a surface-bound species or a surface-adsorbed

D. Factors causing saturation
The saturation of the surface is caused by the chemisorption of the reactant molecules. The saturation of the surface
can be achieved in two ways: either the surface is covered
with a monolayer of the reactant molecules, or the surface
is covered with a monolayer of the adsorbed species. The
saturation of the surface is an important factor in the
self-terminating nature of ALD. The saturation of the surface
can be achieved by the chemisorption of the reactant
molecules, as described in Sec. III A. The saturation of the
surface can also be achieved by the chemisorption of the
adsorbed species, as described in Sec. III B. The saturation
of the surface is therefore a result of the chemisorption
process, and the saturation of the surface is the condition
that allows the self-terminating nature of ALD.
