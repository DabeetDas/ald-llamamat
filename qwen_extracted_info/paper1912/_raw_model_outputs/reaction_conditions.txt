  "evidence": "The initial experiments using alternating pulses of LiN(SiMe3)2 and H2O resulted in films with rather uncontrolled growth and large gradients. These films were in addition highly reactive towards air which made characterization extremely difficult. The uncontrolled growth is likely caused by a reservoir effect where the bulk of the film absorbs water providing a large supply of hydroxide groups in addition to the self limiting surface reaction. Any LiOH thereby formed in the bulk of the film may react with the subsequent pulses of LiN(SiMe3)2 leading to a growth rather limited by the thickness of the deposited film. This finding is also supported by earlier results of Aaltonen. In order to circumvent the problem, a pulse of CO2 was introduced after each water pulse in order to form the stable and non-hygroscopic Li2CO3. The pulse and purge parameters were optimized at a deposition temperature of 186 uC, resulting in an LiN(SiMe3)2 pulse of 4 s and an H2O pulse of 0.25 s in order to obtain surface limited growth. The suggested wide ALD window from the thermal decomposition experiments was indeed observed experimentally. The growth of Li3N was achieved by alternating pulses of LiN(SiMe3)2 and NH3, with the surface reaction involving ligand exchange and self-limiting growth."
