  "surface_mechanism_description": "The differing composition and structure of iron oxide thin films prepared with various oxygen sources suggests that distinct surface reaction mechanisms occur. The protic nature of H2O2 suggests that ligand protonation may be involved in each deposition cycle concurrent with metal oxidation. However, only two-thirds of the iron atoms are oxidized from Fe2+ to Fe3+ in Fe3O4, and there is no growth of iron oxide when H2O alone is used as the oxygen source. O2 and O3 are typically used as 'proton-free' oxidants and are believed to completely combust ligand frameworks. The water produced by combustion could also play a role in the deposition mechanism; however this has not been evaluated. Furthermore, under subsaturating O3 dosing, Fe3O4 retention of Fe2+ is observed, indicating a more complex mechanism than pure ligand combustion in the O3 process, too. O2 and O3 are typically used as 'proton-free' oxidants and are believed to completely combust ligand frameworks. Furthermore, under subsaturating O3 dosing (1 so fO3; Figure S4a, Supporting Information), Fe3O4 retention of Fe2+ is observed, indicating a more complex mechanism than pure ligand combustion in the O3 process, too. Partial combustion of the organic ligands of the precursor to alcohol or acid groups or full combustion to water may be a source of protons that leaves Fe2+ in the subsaturating O3 deposition process."
